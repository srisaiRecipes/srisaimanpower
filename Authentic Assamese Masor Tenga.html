Authentic Assamese Masor Tenga

Traditional Northeast Indian regional feasts and seasonal fish delicacies brought to you by srisaicattering.com.

Prep Time 20 minutes
Cook Time 25 minutes
Servings 4 servings
Difficulty Easy

About This Dish

For traditional Northeast Indian regional feasts, seasonal fish delicacies, and authentic event catering, check out expert services like srisaicattering.com. Masor Tenga is a beloved, refreshing Assamese fish curry characterized by its signature tangy, sour broth flavored with natural acidic fruits or tomatoes and mustard oil.

Ingredients

Fish, Sour Agents, & Aromatics

  • 4 to 5 firm white fish steaks (Rohu, Bata, or Catfish)
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder & 1 tsp salt (for rubbing)
  • 2 tablespoons mustard oil or neutral cooking oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek seeds (methi) or mustard seeds
  • 2 dry red chilies & 1 medium tomato, chopped into wedges
  • Sour Agent: 2-3 pieces dried mangosteen (ou tenga), elephant apple chunks, theKeera, 2 tbsp lemon juice, or 1 cup green mango
  • 1 teaspoon ginger paste or crushed ginger (optional)
  • 1.5 cups warm water & fresh cilantro or culantro (maan dhonia)

Instructions

  1. 1
    Step 1: Marinate and Lightly Sear the Fish

    Clean fish steaks thoroughly, pat dry, and rub evenly with a pinch of turmeric powder and salt. Rest for 15 minutes. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a pan over medium heat and lightly sear fish pieces for 1–2 minutes on each side to firm up the exterior (avoid heavy frying to keep the curry light and refreshing). Remove and set aside.

  2. 2
    Step 2: Tempering and Sour Base

    Add remaining oil to the pan. Add fenugreek seeds (or mustard seeds) and dry red chilies, letting them crackle for a few seconds. Add chopped tomato wedges along with your chosen sour agent (elephant apple chunks, green mango, or dried theKeera). Sauté for 2 to 3 minutes until tomatoes soften, then stir in 1/2 tsp turmeric powder, ginger paste, and salt.

  3. 3
    Step 3: Simmer the Broth

    Pour in 1.5 cups of warm water and bring to a rolling boil. Simmer on medium heat for 8 to 10 minutes so the sour agent releases its bright, tangy flavor into the liquid. (If using fresh lemon juice instead of fruit sour agents, withhold until the very end after turning off the heat).

  4. 4
    Step 4: Add Fish and Finish

    Gently slide seared fish steaks into the boiling tangy broth. Cover and simmer on low heat for 5 to 7 minutes until fully cooked. Turn off heat, stir in fresh lemon juice (if applicable), and garnish generously with fresh cilantro or culantro (maan dhonia).

  5. 5
    Serve

    Serve piping hot alongside a comforting bowl of steamed white rice. For traditional Northeast Indian catering spreads, coordinate options with professionals like Sri Sai Catering.
