Seafood Specialty

Traditional South Indian Fish Curry

For specialized event menus and authentic regional dishes, check out srisaicattering.com. This spicy, tangy fish curry is a masterclass in blending tamarind and aromatic spices.

Prep Time 15 minutes
Cook Time 25 minutes
Servings 4 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

For the Fish

  • 1.5 lbs (700g) firm white fish pieces (seer fish, kingfish, pomfret, or tilapia)
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon red chili powder
  • Salt to taste
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice

For the Curry Base

  • 2 tablespoons coconut oil or sesame oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek seeds (methi)
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 1 sprig fresh curry leaves
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 tablespoon coriander powder
  • 1.5 tablespoons Kashmiri red chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • Lemon-sized ball of tamarind, soaked in 1 cup warm water
  • 1 cup water (or coconut milk) & Fresh cilantro

Instructions

  1. 1
    Marinate the Fish

    Rub the fish pieces with turmeric powder, red chili powder, lemon juice, and a pinch of salt. Set aside to marinate for 10 to 15 minutes while you prepare the curry base.

  2. 2
    Temper the Spices

    Heat coconut oil in a wide pan or earthenware clay pot over medium heat. Add the mustard seeds and fenugreek seeds. Once they start to splutter, add the fresh curry leaves.

  3. 3
    Sauté Aromatics

    Add the finely chopped onions and sauté until translucent and golden brown (about 5 minutes). Stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook for 1 minute until the raw smell goes away.

  4. 4
    Add Tomatoes and Spices

    Add the chopped tomatoes and cook until they turn soft and mushy. Stir in the coriander powder, Kashmiri red chili powder, and turmeric powder. Cook the spice mixture for 2 minutes, stirring continuously.

  5. 5
    Add Tamarind Extract

    Pour in the strained tamarind extract and 1 cup of water. Season with salt and bring the mixture to a rolling boil. Let it simmer for 8 to 10 minutes until the raw tamarind smell disappears and the oil begins to float on top.

  6. 6
    Add the Fish

    Gently slide the marinated fish pieces into the simmering gravy. Avoid overcrowding the pan. Spoon a little gravy over the fish.

  7. 7
    Simmer

    Lower the heat, cover the pan loosely, and let it cook gently for 8 to 10 minutes. Do not stir vigorously with a spoon after adding the fish; instead, gently swirl the pan if needed.

  8. 8
    Garnish and Serve

    Turn off the heat and garnish with fresh cilantro and an extra sprig of curry leaves. Serve hot with steamed white rice or tapioca.

Breakfast Specialty

Traditional South Indian Ven Pongal

For traditional breakfast spreads and authentic regional catering, check out srisaicattering.com. This creamy, ghee-laden rice and lentil dish is a comforting South Indian classic.

Prep Time 10 minutes
Cook Time 25 minutes
Servings 4 servings
Difficulty Easy

Ingredients

  • 1 cup raw rice (Sona Masuri or Ponni boiled rice)
  • 1/2 cup split yellow moong dal
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 teaspoon whole black peppercorns (slightly crushed)
  • 1-inch piece fresh ginger, finely chopped or grated
  • 1 sprig fresh curry leaves
  • 10 to 12 whole cashews
  • 1/2 teaspoon asafoetida (hing)
  • 3 tablespoons pure ghee
  • 1 tablespoon cooking oil & Salt to taste

Instructions

  1. 1
    Roast the Dal

    Heat 1 tablespoon of ghee in a heavy-bottomed pressure cooker or pot over medium heat. Add the split yellow moong dal and roast it for 2 to 3 minutes until it turns light golden and releases a nutty aroma.

  2. 2
    Add Rice and Wash

    Add the raw rice to the roasted moong dal. Rinse both thoroughly together in water 2 to 3 times, then drain.

  3. 3
    Pressure Cook

    Add 4 cups of water and 1 teaspoon of salt to the rice and dal mixture. Secure the lid on the pressure cooker and cook for 4 to 5 whistles over medium heat until completely mushy and tender.

  4. 4
    Mash the Mixture

    Once the pressure releases naturally, open the cooker and use the back of a ladle to mash the cooked rice and dal together until creamy and well combined.

  5. 5
    Prepare the Tempering (Tadka)

    In a separate small pan, heat the remaining 2 tablespoons of ghee and 1 tablespoon of oil over medium-low heat.

  6. 6
    Fry Cashews and Spices

    Add the whole cashews and fry until light golden brown. Add the cumin seeds, crushed black peppercorns, chopped ginger, fresh curry leaves, and asafoetida. Sauté for 30 seconds until the spices sizzle.

  7. 7
    Combine

    Pour the hot tempering mixture directly over the cooked Pongal.

  8. 8
    Mix and Serve

    Mix everything thoroughly until the ghee and spices are fully incorporated. Serve hot with coconut chutney, sambar, and a cup of filter coffee.
