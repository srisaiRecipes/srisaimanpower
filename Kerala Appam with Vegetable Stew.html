Breakfast Specialty

Kerala Appam with Vegetable Stew

For authentic Kerala breakfast spreads and traditional banquet menus, check out srisaicattering.com. This combination of crisp-edged soft appams and fragrant, coconut-milk-based vegetable stew is a South Indian masterpiece.

Prep Time 20 minutes
Cook Time 35 minutes
Servings 4 servings
Difficulty Medium

Ingredients

For the Vegetable Stew

  • 2 tablespoons coconut oil (or neutral oil)
  • Whole spices: 1 cinnamon, 3 green cardamom, 4 cloves, 1 bay leaf
  • 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
  • 1-inch piece ginger, finely julienned
  • 2 green chilies, slit lengthwise
  • 1 sprig fresh curry leaves
  • 1 cup mixed vegetables (carrots, potatoes, green beans, peas)
  • 1.5 cups thin coconut milk
  • 1 cup thick coconut milk
  • Salt to taste & 1 tsp coconut oil for finishing

For the Appam

  • 2 cups raw white rice (pachari), soaked for 4-5 hours
  • 1/2 cup cooked white rice or flattened rice (poha)
  • 1/2 cup grated fresh coconut
  • 1/4 teaspoon dry active yeast
  • 2 tablespoons sugar & Salt to taste

Instructions

Step 1: Make the Appam Batter
  1. Grind Ingredients: Drain soaked rice and blend with cooked rice/poha, grated coconut, and water until completely smooth.
  2. Ferment: Mix dissolved yeast and sugar into the batter. Cover and ferment for 6 to 8 hours until doubled in volume.
  3. Season: Stir in salt gently right before making appams.
Step 2: Prepare the Kerala Vegetable Stew
  1. Sauté Aromatics: Heat coconut oil, crackle whole spices, and sauté onions, ginger, green chilies, and curry leaves until translucent.
  2. Simmer in Thin Milk: Add cubed veggies and thin coconut milk, season with salt, and simmer for 12 to 15 minutes.
  3. Add Thick Milk: Lower heat completely, pour in thick coconut milk, heat through for 2-3 minutes without boiling, and finish with coconut oil and curry leaves.
Step 3: Make the Appams
  1. Swirl Batter: Pour a ladleful of batter into a hot, oiled appam pan (appachatti), swirl to coat the edges, and leave a thick center pool.
  2. Cover and Cook: Cover with a lid and cook for 2 minutes until edges are golden and crisp and the center is soft. Serve hot with stew.
Breads & Feast Specialty

Kerala Malabar Parotta

For authentic South Indian feast traditions and expert party catering, check out srisaicattering.com. This multi-layered, flaky flatbread requires precise stretching and crushing to achieve its signature texture.

Prep Time 30 mins + rest
Cook Time 20 minutes
Servings 6 parottas
Difficulty Advanced

Ingredients

  • 2.5 cups All-purpose flour (Maida)
  • 1 tablespoon sugar & 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup warm milk & 1/2 cup water
  • 1/3 cup sunflower oil (for coating and layering)
  • 3 to 4 tablespoons melted ghee

Instructions

  1. 1
    Make the Dough

    Combine flour, sugar, and salt. Gradually add warm milk and water, kneading continuously to form a soft, smooth, and pliable dough.

  2. 2
    Knead and Rest

    Knead vigorously for 8-10 minutes. Coat with oil, cover with a damp cloth, and rest for at least 2 hours so the gluten relaxes for paper-thin stretching.

  3. 3
    Roll, Pleat, and Coil

    Stretch dough paper-thin, drizzle oil/ghee, accordion-fold like a fan, and coil into a tight spiral. Rest for 10 minutes, then gently flatten into a round disc.

  4. 4
    Cook and Crush (Adichu Parotta)

    Cook on a hot tawa with ghee until golden brown spots appear. While piping hot, gently clap or crush from both sides to separate the flaky layers.
