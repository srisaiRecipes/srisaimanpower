Traditional South Indian Puliyodarai

Authentic temple-style flavor brought to you by srisaicattering.com.

Prep Time 15 minutes
Cook Time 30 minutes
Servings 4 servings
Difficulty Medium

About This Dish

For traditional festival feasts and authentic South Indian temple-style spreads, check out srisaicattering.com. Puliyodarai (Tamarind Rice) is a quintessential South Indian delicacy beloved for its rich, tangy, and spiced profile that tastes even better as it rests.

Ingredients

For the Rice

  • 1.5 cups raw long-grain rice (or Sona Masuri)
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil (gingelly oil)
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • Salt to taste

For the Puliyodarai Powder (Puli Podi)

  • 1 tablespoon coriander seeds
  • 1 tablespoon chana dal (split Bengal gram)
  • 1 tablespoon urad dal (split black gram)
  • 6 to 8 dry red chilies (adjust to spice level)
  • 1 teaspoon black peppercorns
  • 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek seeds (methi)
  • 1 tablespoon white sesame seeds

For the Tamarind Masala (Pulikachal)

  • Lemon-sized ball of tamarind, soaked in 1 cup warm water and extracted for thick juice
  • 3 tablespoons sesame oil (gingelly oil is key)
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 1 teaspoon chana dal and 1 teaspoon urad dal
  • 2 tablespoons raw peanuts or cashews
  • 2 sprigs fresh curry leaves
  • 2 to 3 dry red chilies
  • 1/4 teaspoon asafoetida (hing)
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 tablespoon jaggery (optional)
  • Salt to taste

Instructions

  1. 1
    Cook the Rice

    Cook the rice with slightly less water than usual so the grains remain separate and firm. Spread the cooked hot rice on a wide plate or tray, drizzle with 1 teaspoon of sesame oil, 1/2 teaspoon turmeric, and a pinch of salt. Gently mix and let it cool completely.

  2. 2
    Roast Spices for Powder

    In a dry pan over low heat, dry roast the coriander seeds, chana dal, urad dal, dry red chilies, black peppercorns, and fenugreek seeds until golden and fragrant. In the last 30 seconds, add the sesame seeds and toss until they pop. Let it cool, then grind into a fine powder. Set aside.

  3. 3
    Extract Tamarind Juice

    Squeeze the soaked tamarind thoroughly to extract thick juice and strain out any seeds or fiber.

  4. 4
    Prepare the Pulikachal (Tamarind Base)

    Heat 3 tablespoons of sesame oil in a heavy-bottomed pan or wok over medium heat. Add mustard seeds and let them splutter. Add the chana dal, urad dal, and peanuts (or cashews), frying until golden brown.

  5. 5
    Add Aromatics

    Toss in the curry leaves, dry red chilies, and asafoetida.

  6. 6
    Simmer Tamarind

    Pour in the extracted tamarind juice, turmeric powder, and salt. Bring it to a boil, then reduce the heat to medium-low. Let it simmer, stirring occasionally, until the raw tamarind smell completely vanishes and the oil begins to separate from the sides (about 12 to 15 minutes). Stir in the jaggery if using.

  7. 7
    Add the Spice Powder

    Turn off the heat or lower it completely, and add 2 to 3 tablespoons of the freshly ground Puliyodarai powder. Mix well into the thick tamarind paste (Pulikachal). Let it cool down.

  8. 8
    Mix with Rice

    Add spoonfuls of the prepared Pulikachal to the cooled rice. Mix gently and evenly using a spatula, adjusting the amount of paste to suit your taste.

  9. 9
    Rest and Serve

    Cover and let the Puliyodarai rest for at least 30 minutes to an hour so the flavors absorb fully into the rice. Serve at room temperature with fried papad, vadam, or potato chips.
